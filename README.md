## What is this Project? 

When I began working with Dr. Anastasia Salter to plan the 2024 ELO conference as a Summer Mentoring Fellow at UCF, I felt overwhelmed by the sheer interdisciplinarity of the field of electronic literature and how this changed over time. 

Electronic literature, as the field itself consistently returns to, is definable along many axes (see Scott Rettberg's _Electronic Literature_). 

The proposal of this project is that by mapping where self-described practitioners of "Electronic Literature" appear in publications, at conferences, in collectives, and themselves publish, we could get a sense for where the boundaries of the field are.

Instead of treating those boundaries as hard lines, visualizing these links with force-directed network mapping maps these relationships as open space (in 2D and, with other Obsidian extensions, 3D). 

With this in mind, E-literature exists as a collective of events and archives in-between. Like Antarctica, a massive space divided like a pie chart, from the central cluster of Electronic Literature Conferences (ELO and E-Poetry) you'll find many different territories: 
- Traditional New Media/Electronic Arts Organizations
- Social Studies of Digital Media and Video Games
- Indie Game and Interactive Fiction Developers 
- Internationally-located Archives of Electronic/Digital Literature
- Computer Sciences and Humanities Computing
- Digital Humanities Hubs
- Experimental Literature Repositories
- Hypertext Studios and HTML Directories
- Rhetoric and Composition Scholarship 
- AI, Blockchain, and Web3 arts
- Digital Cultures Labs, Collectives, and Research Groups
- Schools of Practice in the Digital Arts
- Digital Literary Magazines and FanZines 
- Academic Book Series and Imprints about Electronic Literature
- Academic Journals about E-Lit, Digital Narrative, Social Media studies, etc.

As you might find inside this "Little Database" (as Danny Snelson writes), E-literature exists in and among many disciplines revolving around computers and creativity, the humanities and the social sciences. We study stories that circulate in digital spaces, and we write our own. 

This little database is a cross-section: reducing prolific scholars and conference volunteers alike to their contributor's bylines in conference proceedings, journal publications, and "about" pages.
As of Sept. 2026, there are 101,000 outgoing links in this corpus. 
Because of overlap, links, and repetitions, outgoing links represent about 57,000 individuals with bylines in the corpus.
